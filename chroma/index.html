Chroma

See the music. Chroma listens and paints what it hears: every note, chord and beat becomes colour, light and movement.

Put your phone near the music: a speaker, a band, a friend singing. Each note has its own colour. Happy chords glow bright, sad chords go deep, tense chords clash, and every beat sends out a ripple.

🔒 Sound is analysed on your device. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.
⚡ Flashes are limited to 3 per second. Turn on Calm mode in settings for gentler light.

Settings

How sound becomes colour

Chroma follows musical rules, so the same music always gives the same colours. With practice you can learn to read a song from its colours.

Every note has a colour

The 12 notes go round the colour wheel in the order of the circle of fifths, the order musicians use to show which notes sound good together. C is red, G is orange, D is yellow, and so on. This is close to the colours the composer Alexander Scriabin gave to keys.

Notes that sound good together (a fifth apart) are neighbouring colours. The clashing tritone sits opposite, on the complementary colour.

Chords set the mood

MajorBright and warm. Happy, open sound.
MinorDeeper and darker. Sad or serious sound.
SeventhRich, with a clashing edge. Wants to move on.
Major 7thSoft pastel. Dreamy.
DiminishedMurky and grey. Tense, spooky.
AugmentedOver-bright and unstable. Floating, strange.
SusAiry and pale. Waiting to resolve.

A major chord's notes lean towards the warm side of its root colour. A minor chord's notes lean towards the cool side.

The rest of the music

KeyThe background colour. It is the song's home note and changes slowly.
LoudnessBrightness and size. Quiet music glows softly; loud music fills the screen.
DrumsKept apart from the notes. Drums have no pitch, so they have no note colour: they show as white light. Kick drums pulse up from the bottom (and vibrate, if you turn that on), snares ring from the centre, and hi-hats give a few sparks. Drum hits don't change the note colours. To see only the notes, turn off Show drums.
MelodyA glowing ribbon. High notes are higher on the screen and lighter in colour.
TensionClashing notes add the opposite colour and make the shapes shake. When the music resolves, it calms down.
Bright soundsHigh instruments make sparkles.

Visuals

  • Aurora: soft, moving light. Each note has its own place, so calm chords bunch together and clashing chords spread apart.
  • Wheel: the circle of fifths, with chord shapes drawn on it. Good for learning.
  • Wash: the whole screen in one colour. Calm and easy to follow from the corner of your eye.
  • Hands: every note as a coloured bar with its name, moving from right to left. The high part (a pianist's right hand, or the melody) is on top. The low part (the left hand, or the bass) is below, and the drums run between them. The line between the two parts moves to the biggest gap between the notes.

Tips

  • Put the phone close to the speaker or instrument.
  • If the colours hardly move, raise Sensitivity.
  • Install Chroma on your home screen. It works offline.
