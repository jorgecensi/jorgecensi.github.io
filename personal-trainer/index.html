    How it Works

    Tiers

    Core Fitness and Mat Pilates each have their own score from 0 to 90. Every 18 points unlocks the next tier, and the bars on the Progress tab show how far along you are.

    T1Beginner
    T2Novice
    T3Intermediate
    T4Advanced
    T5Elite

    Workouts draw exercises from your current tier and up to two tiers below it, so new challenges mix with familiar moves. Your Level stat is the average of both disciplines.

    The fitness level you pick during first-time setup only sets where those two scores start — beginner at 0, intermediate at 36, advanced at 54. Changing it later in Settings won't reset the tiers you've already earned; only your feedback moves the scores after that.

    Training in balance

    A strong trunk is a balanced one. Hammering the front — the abs — while the posterior chain (glutes and lower back) lags is the classic home-workout trap: it tips the pelvis into an anterior tilt and loads the lower back, the opposite of the posture you're training for.

    So the app keeps a running tally of how much work each region — abs, obliques, lower back and glutes — has had recently (older sessions fade over about a fortnight), and every new workout leans toward whatever you've done least. Train nothing but abs for a week and the plan starts feeding you glute and back work to even you back out.

    The Muscle balance card on the Progress tab shows each region's share of your recent work against an even split, and calls out whatever's lagging. Tap "View body map" on the card to see how your trunk lights up on an anatomical diagram — the warmer the colour, the more recent work that muscle has had. There's nothing to set — just train, and it self-corrects.

    Mobility, on its own track

    Mobility is a third discipline with its own tier and its own balance ledger, kept over five joint areas — hips, hamstrings, t-spine, shoulders and ankles — rather than the four trunk regions. Stretching a hip isn't core training and doesn't pretend to be, so mobility work never moves your muscle balance and never lights up the body map.

    On the Today tab you pick the shape of the session: Core is the classic circuit, Mixed adds a short mobility block after the work and before the stretches, and Mobility is a gentle stretch day with longer holds and no circuit. Only the disciplines a session actually trained take your feedback afterwards.

    How check-ins work

    A check-in is an optional six-minute battery of self-tests, meant to be run every five weeks or so. The core half is four max-effort holds — plank, side plank on each side, a back-extension hold and a glute-bridge hold — timed until your form breaks. The mobility half is three measured reaches, and needs a tape measure; you can skip it.

    These are endurance and range self-tests, not clinical strength tests. The back-extension hold is a Sørensen proxy, not the strapped clinical version, and you are the one judging when your form broke. So read two things and ignore everything else: your own trend from one check-in to the next, and your left-versus-right symmetry. Those are robust. Population percentiles for these tests come from small samples and would be false precision here, which is why you won't find a grade anywhere in the app.

    For the trend to mean anything, retest the same way each time — same surface, same form standard, same time of day if you can. Results feed the generator: a region that tests weak gets pulled forward in the next few sessions on top of the usual volume balance, and the stiffest joint area steers your mobility blocks.

    Your feedback drives it

    After every workout you say how it felt, and both scores adjust:

    Too easy+8 points
    Just right+4 points
    Too hard−5 points

    Honest answers keep the plan at the right pace — sandbagging just makes next time too easy.

    What actually gets harder
    Tougher exercisesunlock at higher tiers
    Reps & hold timesgrow roughly every 15 points
    Rest between exercises20s → 15s (T2) → 12s (T4)

    Each exercise has its own cap, so doses grow to a sensible limit rather than forever.

    Streak

    Finish a workout within 48 hours of your last one and the streak keeps counting. Miss the window and it restarts at 1 — consistency beats intensity.

    How the Generator Works

    The shape of a session
    Warm-up3 easy moves
    💪 Main circuitCore + Pilates, alternating
    🧘 Mobility2–4 moves on a Mixed day
    Cool-down3 stretches

    Your session length sets the time budget, then the main circuit is filled with as many exercises as fit. Pick 40 minutes and the circuit repeats for a second round instead of just adding more moves.

    The session type you pick on Today reshapes this: Core drops the mobility block, and Mobility drops the circuit instead and spends that whole budget on longer-held joint work. Auto — the default — picks one of the three for you from how long it's been since you last stretched, whether a check-in flagged a stiff joint, and the length you chose; the line under the Generate button always says which it landed on and why.

    Picking the exercises

    Each pick comes from your current tier in that discipline (Core or Pilates), down to two tiers below it — so it's mostly familiar with a few fresh challenges mixed in. Within the session the list stays balanced across ab, oblique, back and glute focus, and it also leans toward whichever of those regions your recent training has neglected (see “Training in balance” under How Progression Works). If you've run a check-in, whatever tested weakest gets pulled forward on top of that. It skips whatever you did in your very last session so two in a row never feel identical.

    The mobility block is picked the same way, but from its own five joint areas and its own tier — the least-opened area goes first, and a check-in's stiffest reading pulls harder still.

    Today's twist

    Every workout gets a random theme, never the same one twice in a row: a focus day (abs, obliques, or glutes & back), an endurance day with doses up ~20%, a speedy day with shorter rests, a finisher with one max-effort hold at the end, or the classic balanced mix.

    Rest between exercises

    Higher tiers rest less between moves — 20s at Tier 1, down to 12s at Tier 4+ — and a speedy twist shortens it further. Warm-up and cool-down moves get a short breather too, just enough to get set up for the next one.

    Not feeling this mix?

    Tap 🔀 on the preview screen to reshuffle before you start — as many times as you like, no penalty. Once you're training, your post-workout feedback (too easy / just right / too hard) is what actually moves the difficulty needle — see How Progression Works for that part.
